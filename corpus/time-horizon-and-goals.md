# Time Horizon and Goals

Time horizon is how long an investor expects to hold an investment before needing the money. It is
one of the most important inputs to an investment decision, because it shapes how much risk is
reasonable to take. Matching investments to specific goals, such as a near-term purchase versus
retirement decades away, is the heart of goal-based investing.

A longer time horizon generally allows for more risk. An investor saving for a goal many years away
has more time to recover from market downturns, so a larger share of stocks may be appropriate. For
a goal only a year or two away, preserving the money and keeping it accessible usually matters more
than chasing growth, so safer and more liquid choices tend to fit better.

Time horizon works together with an investor's goals and tolerance for risk to guide an appropriate
asset allocation. Because circumstances and timelines change, it is common to revisit the plan
periodically and adjust as a goal draws closer.
