# Stress-Testing a Decision Process

A decision process that works in calm markets has not really been tested. The conditions that do the
most damage, sharp crashes and sudden shocks, are rare, so they are easy to leave out of an
evaluation. A process should be checked against those hard scenarios before they happen, not only
after.

One practical method is to replay a historical shock through the same logic that makes decisions
today. Running a past crash through the process can reveal a flaw on paper, at no cost, that would
otherwise show up only when real money is on the line. Finding the weakness in a simulation is far
cheaper than finding it in a live drawdown.

This kind of test depends on the process being reproducible. If the decision logic is deterministic
and can be re-run on old data exactly as it runs today, historical stress tests are possible and
trustworthy. If it cannot be replayed, there is no reliable way to know how it would have behaved
when it mattered most.
