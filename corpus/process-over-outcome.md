# Process Over Outcome

In investing, a single good or bad result says very little about whether the decision behind it was
sound. Randomness is large, so a good decision can lead to a poor outcome and a reckless one can be
rewarded. Judging yourself only by the latest result teaches the wrong lessons.

A better standard is to judge the process. Was the reasoning consistent and stated in advance, or
rationalized after the fact? Was the rule explicit enough that someone else could apply it and get
the same answer? A process that is vague or shifts to fit whatever just happened cannot be evaluated
or improved.

One practical way to protect a process is to keep the deciding logic deterministic and inspectable,
and to separate the decision itself from any after-the-fact explanation of it. When the logic is
written down and repeatable, it can be audited, tested, and corrected. When it lives only as
intuition narrated after the outcome is known, it cannot.
