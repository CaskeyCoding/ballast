# Dollar-Cost Averaging

Dollar-cost averaging is the practice of investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, for
example a set sum every month, regardless of what the market is doing. When prices are high your
fixed amount buys fewer shares, and when prices are low it buys more. Over time this can lower the
average price you pay per share compared with investing the same total all at once at an unlucky
moment.

The main benefit is behavioral. By investing automatically on a schedule, you avoid trying to time
the market, which is notoriously difficult even for professionals. It removes the temptation to wait
for the "perfect" moment and helps you keep contributing during downturns, which is exactly when
shares are cheaper.

Dollar-cost averaging does not guarantee a profit and does not protect against loss in a falling
market. If markets rise steadily over the long run, investing a lump sum earlier would often have
produced a higher return. The strategy's real value is consistency and discipline: it turns
investing into a steady habit rather than a series of anxious decisions. Many people practice it
automatically through regular contributions to a retirement plan.
