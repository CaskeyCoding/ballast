# Intellectual Honesty in Investing

The hardest discipline in investing is changing your mind in the open. When a rule or model turns out
to be wrong, the tempting response is to quietly adjust it and move on, which hides the mistake and
the lesson. The honest response is to revise it openly and record why it changed.

Several smaller habits are part of the same posture. Evaluate results net of costs rather than gross,
because fees and trading frictions are real and a gross figure flatters the truth. State uncertainty
plainly instead of projecting false confidence. Prefer evidence over a compelling story, since a
narrative can feel convincing long after the data has stopped supporting it.

The common thread is not fooling yourself. An investor who measures honestly, admits error quickly,
and resists the pull of a good story is harder to surprise. The goal is not to be right every time,
which is impossible, but to keep a clear and truthful account of what is known and what is not.
