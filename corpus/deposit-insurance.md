# Deposit Insurance

Deposit insurance protects money held in accounts at insured banks if the bank fails. In the United
States, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, or FDIC, insures deposits at member banks. This
backing is a major reason that everyday bank accounts are considered a safe place to hold cash for
near-term needs and emergencies.

Coverage applies up to a limit, which is set by law at 250,000 dollars per depositor, per insured
bank, per ownership category. Spreading money across ownership categories or across separate insured
banks can increase the total amount that is covered. The insurance is automatic at a member bank, so
depositors do not have to apply for it.

It is important to know what deposit insurance does not cover. It protects deposit products such as
checking accounts, savings accounts, and certificates of deposit. It does not cover investments such
as stocks, bonds, or mutual funds, even when they are bought through a bank, because those products
can lose value and are not deposits.
