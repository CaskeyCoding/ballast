# Credit and Debt

Credit is the ability to borrow money and repay it later, usually with interest. Used carefully, it
can help people manage large purchases over time. The cost of credit is the interest and fees, and
that cost varies a great deal depending on the type of borrowing and the borrower's credit history.

A credit report is a record of how a person has borrowed and repaid, and a credit score summarizes
that history into a number. Lenders use these to decide whether to lend and at what interest rate, so
a stronger history generally means access to lower-cost credit. Checking one's own reports for
errors is a sensible habit.

High-interest debt, such as a balance carried on a credit card, can compound against a borrower in
the same way that investment returns compound in their favor. Because of this, paying down
high-interest debt is often one of the most reliable uses of money. Building a small emergency fund
first can also keep an unexpected expense from turning into new high-interest debt.
