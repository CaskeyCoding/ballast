# Risk and Return

In investing, risk and potential return are linked. Investments with the potential for higher
returns generally carry a higher risk of loss, while lower-risk investments tend to offer lower
returns. There is no investment that is both completely safe and high-returning; be skeptical of any
pitch that promises one.

Different assets sit at different points on this spectrum. Cash and government-backed savings carry
low risk and low expected return. Bonds sit in the middle. Stocks have historically offered higher
long-term returns but with larger swings in value along the way. The chance that an investment loses
value, especially in the short term, is the price you pay for the chance of higher long-term growth.

How much risk you should take depends on your goals, your time horizon, and your personal comfort
with volatility. A longer time horizon generally allows more tolerance for short-term ups and downs,
because there is more time to recover from declines. Risk can be managed through diversification and
asset allocation, but it cannot be removed entirely. Understanding your own risk tolerance helps you
hold steady through market declines instead of selling at the worst time.
