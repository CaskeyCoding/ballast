# Compound Interest

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money and on the interest that money has
already earned. Over time, your earnings generate their own earnings, and growth accelerates. This
is why starting to invest early is so powerful: time is the main ingredient that lets compounding
work.

A simple way to gauge compounding is the "Rule of 72": divide 72 by your annual rate of return to
estimate the number of years it takes to roughly double your money. At a 6 percent annual return,
money doubles in about twelve years; at 8 percent, in about nine years.

The same mechanism works against you with high-interest debt, such as credit card balances, where
interest compounds on what you owe. Paying down high-interest debt can be one of the most reliable
"returns" available. For investing, the practical lessons are to start early, contribute regularly,
reinvest earnings, and give the money long periods of time to grow. Small, consistent contributions
can outgrow larger, later ones because they compound for longer.
