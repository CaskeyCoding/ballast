# Emergency Funds

An emergency fund is money set aside in a safe, easily accessible account to cover unexpected
expenses such as a medical bill, a car repair, or a period without income. A common guideline is to
hold three to six months of essential living expenses, though the right amount depends on your job
stability and obligations.

An emergency fund matters before investing for two reasons. First, it keeps a surprise expense from
forcing you to sell investments at a bad time, for example during a market decline, to raise cash.
Second, it provides peace of mind that lets you stay invested for the long term instead of reacting
to every short-term need. Because this money may be needed at any moment, it belongs in a low-risk,
liquid place such as a savings account, not in stocks.

A sensible order of priorities for many people is: cover essential expenses, build a starter
emergency fund, pay down high-interest debt, capture any employer retirement match, then invest for
long-term goals. Investing works best when it is money you will not need to touch for years, so the
emergency fund is what makes a long-term investing plan durable.
