# Avoiding Investment Fraud

Investment fraud often shares a few recognizable warning signs. Promises of high returns with little
or no risk are a classic red flag, because in legitimate investing higher potential returns come
with higher risk. Pressure to act immediately, claims that an opportunity is guaranteed or cannot
lose, and sellers who are not properly registered are all reasons for caution.

Some schemes follow well-known patterns. A Ponzi scheme pays earlier investors with money from new
investors rather than from real profits, so it collapses when new money slows. Affinity fraud
targets members of a group who trust one another, using that trust to spread the scam. Unsolicited
offers and complex, hard-to-verify strategies also deserve extra scrutiny.

A few habits reduce the risk of being defrauded. Investors can check whether a person or firm is
registered with regulators, ask for information in writing, and avoid being rushed. A simple rule of
thumb remains useful: if an opportunity sounds too good to be true, it very likely is.
