# Index Funds

An index fund is a mutual fund or exchange-traded fund (ETF) designed to track the performance of a
market index, such as a broad index of large U.S. companies. Rather than paying managers to pick
individual stocks, an index fund simply holds the securities in its target index in roughly the same
proportions. This is called passive management.

Because passive management requires less research and trading, index funds typically charge much
lower fees than actively managed funds. Over long periods, those lower costs compound into a
meaningful difference in net returns. Many actively managed funds fail to beat their benchmark index
after fees, which is part of why index funds have become popular.

An index fund gives instant diversification across all the securities in its index in a single
purchase. It will not beat the market, because it is the market, minus a small fee. It also will not
protect you from broad market declines: when the index falls, the fund falls with it. Index funds
suit investors who want low-cost, diversified, long-term exposure without trying to outguess the
market.
