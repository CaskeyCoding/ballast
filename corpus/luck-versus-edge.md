# Luck Versus Edge

A strong track record can come from skill or from luck, and over short periods the two are hard to
tell apart. Markets are noisy, so even a process with no real advantage will sometimes produce
impressive runs. This is why a good result, on its own, is weak evidence.

The problem gets worse the more things you try. If you test many strategies or many variations, the
best one will look excellent simply because you took the maximum over a lot of noise. Honest
evaluation has to account for how many attempts were made. Statistical tools such as the Deflated
Sharpe Ratio (Bailey and Lopez de Prado) adjust a performance figure for the number of trials and
for non-normal returns, which lowers the illusion created by searching.

The humble default is to assume a result is luck until the evidence is strong and survives data the
strategy never saw. Treating apparent edge as guilty until proven innocent is uncomfortable, but it
is the posture that keeps an investor from acting on noise.
