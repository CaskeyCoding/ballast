# Diversification

Diversification means spreading your money across many different investments rather than
concentrating it in one. The idea is captured by the phrase "do not put all your eggs in one
basket." When you hold a variety of investments, a loss in any single one has a smaller effect on
your overall portfolio.

Diversification works because different assets often do not move in the same direction at the same
time. When some investments fall, others may hold steady or rise, smoothing your total return over
time. A portfolio might diversify across asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash), across industries and
company sizes, and across geographies (domestic and international).

Diversification does not guarantee a profit or protect fully against loss in a declining market. It
is a tool for managing risk, not eliminating it. Broadly diversified funds, such as index funds,
give many investors low-cost access to a wide basket of securities in a single purchase. The right
mix depends on your goals, time horizon, and tolerance for risk.
