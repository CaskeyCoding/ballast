# Retirement Accounts

Retirement accounts are designed to encourage long-term saving by offering tax advantages. A 401(k)
is an employer-sponsored plan, and employers sometimes match part of what an employee contributes,
which is additional money toward retirement. An individual retirement arrangement, or IRA, is an
account a person opens on their own. Both come in traditional and Roth versions.

The traditional and Roth versions differ in when taxes are paid. Traditional contributions are
generally made before tax, and withdrawals in retirement are taxed. Roth contributions are made with
money that has already been taxed, and qualified withdrawals in retirement are generally tax-free.
Which is better depends on an individual's situation, including expected future tax rates.

These accounts have annual contribution limits and rules about withdrawals, including potential
penalties for taking money out early. The specific dollar limits are set by the IRS and are adjusted
periodically, so an investor should check the current year's figures rather than rely on a fixed
number. The accounts hold investments such as funds; they are containers, not investments
themselves.
