# Asset Allocation

Asset allocation is how you divide your portfolio among the major asset classes, primarily stocks,
bonds, and cash. Because these classes respond differently to market conditions, your allocation is
one of the biggest drivers of both your expected return and the size of your portfolio's swings.

A portfolio weighted toward stocks has higher long-term growth potential but larger short-term
declines. A portfolio weighted toward bonds and cash is steadier but grows more slowly. Many
investors shift their allocation over time, holding more stocks when their time horizon is long and
gradually moving toward bonds and cash as a goal such as retirement approaches.

Over time, market movements push your allocation away from its targets: if stocks rise, they grow to
a larger share of the portfolio than you intended. Rebalancing means periodically buying and selling
to return to your target mix. This enforces a discipline of trimming what has risen and adding to
what has lagged. There is no single correct allocation; the right one depends on your goals, time
horizon, and risk tolerance, and it should be reviewed as those change.
