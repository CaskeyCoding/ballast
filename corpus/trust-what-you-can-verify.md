# Trust What You Can Verify

Not all evidence deserves equal weight. Some inputs to a decision are well measured and have held up
under scrutiny, while others are noisy, hard to verify, or based on a small sample. Treating all of
them as equally reliable is a common way to reach an overconfident conclusion.

A useful discipline is to draw an explicit trust boundary. Let only the high-trust, verifiable
evidence drive a consequential decision, and keep the weaker signals as context to watch rather than
as reasons to act. Naming which inputs are allowed to carry weight, and which are only being
monitored, prevents a weak or unproven signal from quietly steering an important conclusion.

This is not about ignoring information. It is about being honest regarding how much each piece of
information has earned. As a signal proves itself over time, it can move across the boundary and gain
a vote. Until then, watching it costs nothing, while acting on it can cost a great deal.
