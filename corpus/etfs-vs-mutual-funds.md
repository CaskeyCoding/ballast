# Mutual Funds and ETFs

Mutual funds and exchange-traded funds, or ETFs, are both pooled investments. Many investors put
money in together, and the fund uses it to hold a basket of securities such as stocks or bonds. This
lets an investor own a slice of many holdings through a single purchase, which is a convenient way
to diversify.

The main difference is how they trade. A mutual fund is bought and sold once per day at a price
based on the value of its holdings, called the net asset value. An ETF trades throughout the day on
an exchange like a stock, so its price can move during market hours. Both can follow an index
passively or be actively managed, and both charge fees that reduce returns over time.

When choosing between them, investors often look at the fund's costs, what it holds, how it is
managed, and any trading or tax differences. As with all investments, a fund can lose value, and
past performance does not guarantee future results.
